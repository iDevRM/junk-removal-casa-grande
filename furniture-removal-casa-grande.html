What Does Furniture Removal Involve?

A two-person crew walks the piece, checks the exit path, and disassembles anything that will not clear a doorway. Bed frames come apart, sectionals separate, and table legs come off. Doorframes and tile get protected before anything moves.

From there the piece goes onto a dolly or moving straps and out to the trailer. Nothing gets dragged across your floor and nothing gets wedged in a hallway on the way out.

Once it is loaded, the piece gets sorted. Clean upholstery and solid wood go to a donation drop. Broken frames and stained mattresses go to disposal. You get a broom-swept space back either way.

Why Do Mattresses Cost Extra to Get Rid Of?

Mattresses are mostly foam, fiber and springs bonded together, which makes them expensive to process and nearly impossible to compact. Most transfer stations charge a per-unit fee to accept one, and many donation centers refuse used mattresses outright for sanitation reasons.

That fee gets passed through to you no matter who hauls it. The difference is whether it shows up as a line item after the fact or is already inside the quote. Ours is inside the quote.

Arizona heat compounds the problem. A mattress or upholstered chair stored in an uncooled Casa Grande garage through a summer becomes a pest and odor issue quickly, which is why the item is usually worse by the time someone calls than it was when they first meant to deal with it.

Donation vs. Disposal: Which Applies to Your Piece?

Donation works when the piece is structurally sound, clean and free of pet damage, tears or heavy stains. Frames that still sit level, dining sets, dressers and solid-wood pieces usually qualify. This is the cheaper path and we prefer it.

Disposal is the answer for broken frames, water damage, mold, pest exposure or anything a donation center will refuse at the door. Trying to donate a piece that gets rejected on arrival means paying for two trips instead of one.

We make that call when the crew sees the piece, not over the phone. If it can be donated, it gets donated.
